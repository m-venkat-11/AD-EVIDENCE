 AD-EVIDENCE
 The Evidence Layer for AI-Era Advertising
NEW SOLUTION & FEATURE SPECIFICATION
Core idea: Do not verify an advertisement only as one piece of content. Identify the important claims inside it, give
each claim a persistent identity, connect it to evidence from multiple sources, detect conflicts and changes, and make
the evidence understandable to brands and consumers.
 “Don’t just tell people what to believe. Show them the evidence behind the claim.”
1. The New Solution
AD-EVIDENCE is a claim-centered verification platform for commercial advertising. When a user submits an
advertisement, product page, marketing document, influencer promotion, or similar content, the system identifies
meaningful claims such as battery life, price, warranty, performance, certification, delivery promise, or comparative
wording.
Each claim is connected to evidence rather than receiving only a black-box trust score. The platform creates a
persistent Claim Evidence Graph connecting the product, claim, advertisement, sources, evidence, conditions,
dates and later observations.
Core flow
ADVERTISEMENT → CLAIM EXTRACTION → PRODUCT IDENTITY → EVIDENCE COLLECTION → CROSS-SOURCE CHECK →
CLAIM PASSPORT → EXPLAINABLE RESULT
2. Claim Passport
A Claim Passport is a persistent record for one important commercial claim. Instead of checking the same claim from
scratch every time it appears, new appearances can be linked to the existing claim when product, meaning and
context match.

4. Cross-Source Conflict Detection
The system compares the claim with several sources and highlights disagreement. It should not automatically declare
a source “true” or “false”; it should show the evidence and explain the conflict.

Example result: “The advertisement claims 50 hours, while the current official specification states up to 40 hours.
Independent evidence reports a lower result under stated conditions. A conflict was detected; review the source
conditions before drawing a final conclusion.”
5. Context-Aware Verification
A number alone is not enough. Claims often contain conditions. The system should compare value, unit, condition,
time, model/version, region and test method where available.
Example: the official document says “up to 50 hours with ANC off”, while an advertisement only says “50-hour
battery life.” The number matches, but the condition is missing. The system should flag Context Missing instead of
automatically calling the claim false.
6. Time-Aware Verification
Prices, discounts, specifications, warranties, subscriptions and features can change. AD-EVIDENCE records when
evidence was observed and can compare historical and current states.
Example: ■4,999 may have been valid in July but the current price may be ■5,499. The system should explain that
the lower price was observed historically rather than blindly calling the old advertisement false.
7. Consumer Evidence
Consumer input should not become another star-rating website. A consumer observation can be structured as a
claim, observed value, conditions, date and optional supporting material.

Example: a consumer reports 28 hours with ANC enabled and high volume. Repeated observations can reveal a

8. Brand Response and Dispute Layer
Brands should be able to respond to evidence without deleting conflicting evidence. For example, a company may
explain that an independent test used conditions different from its official test.
 Claim → Evidence → Conflict → Brand response → Updated evidence
9. Track Claims Across the Advertising Ecosystem
The same claim may appear in Instagram ads, YouTube videos, influencer posts, retailer listings, company websites,
brochures or AI shopping recommendations. When the system establishes that they refer to the same product and
claim, they can be linked to the same Claim Passport.
This turns AD-EVIDENCE from a one-time ad checker into a system that can monitor how a commercial claim travels,
changes, gains evidence or becomes outdated.

11. AI and Provenance
AI such as Gemini can help understand advertisements, images and documents, extract claims, match products,
summarize evidence and identify possible relationships. But AI is not the final judge.
The core verification layer combines AI with deterministic checks where possible: numerical comparison, price
comparison, dates, model/version and conditions. Provenance signals such as C2PA can be recorded when present,
but missing provenance should not automatically mean that content is fake or AI-generated.

13. What Makes AD-EVIDENCE Different?
 Claim-centered: the claim is the persistent object, not just the advertisement.
 Evidence graph: sources and relationships remain connected.
 Conflict detection: disagreements between official, retailer, independent and consumer evidence are surfaced.
 Context-aware: conditions, model/version, region, time and test method can be considered.
 Time-aware: outdated prices and specifications can be detected.
 Claim Passport: the same claim can be followed across ads and platforms.
 Two-sided: one evidence layer serves marketing teams and consumers.
 Explainable: users see the claim, sources and reason for the result.
 AI-assisted, not AI-dependent: AI helps understand content; the evidence system is the core product.
Core USP
 “Don’t verify the advertisement once. Verify the claim wherever it appears.”
An advertisement can change or disappear, but a commercial claim can travel across websites, marketplaces, social
media, influencer content and AI shopping experiences. AD-EVIDENCE is designed to follow that claim and keep its
evidence connected.

17. Recommended Result Categories
Supported — evidence supports the claim.
Contradicted — evidence conflicts with the claim.
Context Missing — important conditions are absent or unclear.
Outdated — the evidence or claim is no longer current.
Insufficient Evidence — not enough reliable evidence was found.
Conflicting Sources — credible sources disagree and the system should show the disagreement.
