1. Executive Summary and Problem Statement
The problem. Generative AI is changing how marketing content is produced. Advertisers can create large
volumes of copy, images, video and variants quickly, but the ability to generate persuasive content does
not automatically guarantee that the content is accurate, supported by evidence, consistent with the
brand, or transparently labeled. IAB research reported that 70% of surveyed U.S. advertising executives
had experienced at least one AI-related advertising incident, including hallucinations, bias or off-brand
content; 40% said they had to pause or pull ads, over a third reported brand damage or PR issues, and
nearly 30% had to conduct internal audits. [R1]
Consumer-side problem. The same content can reach consumers who have little practical way to inspect
the evidence behind a claim. At the same time, AI is becoming a shopping input: BCG reported a 35%
increase in shopping-related GenAI use between February and November 2025 and found more than 60%
of surveyed consumers expressed high trust in GenAI results. [R4] This creates a new trust surface between
advertising, AI-assisted discovery and purchasing.
Proposed solution. AD-EVIDENCE is not an AI judge that emits a single trust score. It is a specialized
verification layer. The system extracts atomic claims from marketing content, identifies the product,
retrieves evidence from multiple source types, preserves source metadata and time, detects support or
conflicts, checks media provenance and applicable disclosure logic, and produces an explainable
claim-by-claim report.
Two-sided model. On the brand side, the system can be used before publication to catch unsupported or
conflicting claims. On the consumer side, the same evidence layer can be used to verify an advertisement
before purchase. A public product evidence page can expose the subset of evidence that is safe to share.
Core thesis: the product is not the LLM. The product is the persistent claim-and-evidence network that can
use GPT/Gemini or any other model underneath it.

3. Product Vision and Unique Positioning
Project title: AD-EVIDENCE
Subtitle: The Evidence Layer for AI-Era Advertising
Tagline: See the claim. See the evidence.
AD-EVIDENCE should be understood as a specialized trust infrastructure layer for commercial claims. It sits
between content generation/publication and human decision-making. It is not intended to decide whether a
consumer should buy a product, and it is not intended to replace legal/compliance teams. Its job is to make
the evidence behind marketing claims visible, traceable, comparable and time-aware.
The core design principle is simple: the system is claim-centric, not ad-centric. An advertisement is a
container of claims. A claim can appear in multiple advertisements, retailer pages, product sheets, reviews
and consumer reports. Once that claim becomes a persistent object, the system can connect and compare
all of those appearances.

The long-term vision is an advertising evidence network where a product has a living evidence record,
advertisements reference claims, brands maintain authoritative records, independent sources can
corroborate or challenge claims, retailers provide market observations, and consumers contribute
structured real-world observations.
Unique positioning hypothesis: Existing enterprise platforms already provide pieces of brand governance
and marketing compliance. Adobe GenStudio applies brand rules and validation; Persado offers
compliance analysis, risk profiles and audit trails. [R8][R9] AD-EVIDENCE should therefore differentiate
itself by centering on a shared claim graph that serves both the brand and the consumer, with evidence
lineage and cross-source conflict visibility as first-class objects. This is a positioning hypothesis, not a
claim that no competitor has adjacent features.

One trust engine, multiple surfaces
Marketing / brand team. Typical questions: "Can we publish this?", "Which claims need support?", "Did the
generated copy strengthen a product claim?", "Does this creative violate our tone?", "What evidence will
we need if challenged?"
Consumer. Typical questions: "Does this advertisement match official product information?", "What exactly
is this ad claiming?", "Is the price current?", "Is this product image representative?", "What evidence
supports the claim?", "Was AI used in a material part of the creative?"
Independent reviewer / analyst. A third persona can inspect conflicts and evidence trails without being a
marketer or consumer. This is useful for internal QA, journalists, researchers or marketplace trust teams.
Important product boundary: the consumer report should explain evidence and uncertainty, not issue a
blanket "buy / do not buy" decision. That keeps the product centered on evidence rather than becoming a
recommendation engine.

6. Intelligent Verification System Design
The verification system should be a hybrid reasoning pipeline. Large language models are good at
interpretation and ambiguity; deterministic software is better for exact numerical/date/unit logic;
provenance systems are better for media history; the graph is better for relationship and lineage questions.
The components should work together rather than allowing a single model to make the final call on
everything.

Intelligent verification pipeline
Detailed stages:
1. Input intake - Accept text, screenshot, image, video, PDF, ad URL, product URL, barcode/GTIN or
brand-uploaded document. Store the original asset and a hash for traceability.
2. Content normalization - OCR visible text, extract PDF text/tables, transcribe video/audio, preserve
layout/asset IDs, and create a normalized content representation.
3. Product identity resolution - Resolve brand/product/model/variant and optionally GTIN. If identity is
uncertain, stop short of definitive verification.

4. Atomic claim extraction - Split the advertisement into small testable assertions: numeric specs,
comparative claims, superlatives, price, warranty, performance, testimonial, visual representation and
disclosure-related statements.
5. Claim normalization - Map wording variants to common attributes and units. Example: "50 hrs", "50-hour
battery", and "50 hours playtime" can map to battery_duration = 50h while retaining the original wording.
6. Evidence retrieval - Retrieve relevant official, independent, retailer, regulatory and other sources. Search
is used for discovery; the system stores source metadata and the exact evidence used.
7. Evidence classification - Classify source type, directness, date, product scope, region and test conditions.
This prevents a retailer listing from being treated exactly like a lab report.
8. Claim-evidence comparison - Run numeric/unit/date comparisons, semantic comparison, context
alignment, source conflict detection and condition checks.
9. Media provenance analysis - Inspect C2PA Content Credentials when present and supported AI
provenance/watermark signals. Absence of provenance is not treated as proof of fabrication.
10. Verification state - Assign Supported, Contradicted, Insufficient Evidence, Context-Dependent, Outdated
or Identity Unconfirmed.
11. Explanation generation - Generate a human-readable report from structured findings and linked
evidence, not from free-form model intuition.
12. Audit + graph update - Store the verification event, evidence snapshot/version, model version, rule
version, timestamps and reviewer action.
13. Re-verification - Trigger re-checks when evidence becomes stale, product data changes, a brand
updates claims, or a consumer/retailer conflict cluster grows.

8. AI Architecture and Model Responsibilities
The AI architecture should deliberately separate understanding tasks from decision rules. Gemini is a
strong initial choice because the current Gemini API supports multimodal document understanding,
structured outputs and Google Search grounding. [R10][R11][R12] The architecture should remain
model-agnostic so a future implementation can swap or ensemble models.
AI / ML UNDERSTANDING LAYER
claim extraction - semantic normalization - evidence retrieval - ambiguous comparison - explanation
DETERMINISTIC VERIFICATION LAYER
numbers - units - dates - price deltas - source rules - temporal freshness - contradiction rules
structured findings in / results out
Use AI for ambiguity; use code for certainty
Recommended AI workers / tool roles:
Content Analyst - Extract text, entities, product names, claims, numbers, comparisons, testimonials and
visible disclosure signals from text/image/video/PDF inputs.
Product Identity Resolver - Map observed product names/model numbers/GTINs to a canonical product
record. Use deterministic identifiers where available and AI for fuzzy matching.

Evidence Researcher - Use Google Search grounding, URL context and approved source connectors to find

Claim Analyst - Compare an atomic claim with retrieved evidence. Handle semantic equivalence,

Provenance Analyst - Inspect C2PA metadata and supported AI provenance signals; classify provenance as

Structured output requirement: use a strict schema for every AI stage. For example, claim extraction

Schema-based structured outputs and can combine structured outputs with tools in supported current
models. [R11]
Search grounding: Gemini Google Search grounding can automatically search, process and cite web
information. We should use that capability to discover evidence, then independently persist the evidence
records and apply our own verification logic. [R10]
9. Deterministic Verification Engine
This is the layer that prevents the product from being a generic LLM wrapper. A deterministic engine should
handle problems where the answer can be computed or ruled by an explicit rule.
Rule class
Example
Numeric comparison
50h advertised vs 40h official -> conflict candidate.
Units
2.5 kg vs 2500 g -> equivalent after normalization.
Ranges
"up to 50h" is not equivalent to "guaranteed 50h"; claim modality is preserved.
Dates
A price from three months ago should not be treated as current without a freshness
check.
Region
₹ price in India is distinct from a U.S. price; region is part of the claim context.
Variant
Model A Pro is not automatically the same product as Model A.
Comparatives
"Best", "#1", "industry-leading" require defined comparison scope and supporting
evidence.
Conditional claims
"Up to 10 hours with ANC off" should not be compared as if it were an unconditional
10-hour claim.
Duplicate claims
Repeated claims across multiple ads should link to one normalized claim where
product/scope/time match.
Freshness
Price/availability can expire quickly; certifications and specs have different review
cadences.
Do not use one giant "trust score". A single number compresses too much uncertainty. If the UI needs a
summary, present counts and categories: 4 supported, 1 contradicted, 2 insufficient evidence, 1 outdated.
The detail view should always show the evidence behind each item.

A result should always answer four questions: What did the advertisement claim? What evidence was
checked? What did the evidence say? Why did the system choose this status?
Example:

Official source: "Up to 40 hours."
Status: CONTRADICTED / STRENGTHENED CLAIM.

should show the original evidence and ask for human review.
The product should also expose conditions. A battery test can differ based on volume, codec, ANC state,
temperature or workload. A context-aware system should retain those conditions rather than flattening all
results into one universal figure.
11. Data Sources and Acquisition Strategy
1. Brand-provided documents. PDFs, manuals, product spec sheets, approved claims, warranty documents,
internal brand guidelines and approved media. Gemini can process PDFs using native vision and can
extract structured information; the current API documentation says PDF files up to 50 MB or 1,000 pages
are supported, with Files API recommended for repeated/larger interactions. [R12]
2. Official websites. Use the manufacturer's official product pages as an important source for current public
specifications, identity and approved product language. Store retrieval timestamp and content
hash/snapshot for auditability.
3. GS1 / product identity. Verified by GS1 contains company information and basic product information for
more than 300 million products, including brand name, product description, category, product image URL,
net content and links to other data sources. Access to some advanced services depends on GS1
membership and member organization access, so production integration must be validated for the intended
market. [R13]
4. Retailer / merchant data. Use official APIs, merchant feeds or permitted pages for price, availability and
seller claims. For example, Google Merchant API supports product, inventory, promotion and review-related
merchant data; it is not a universal public retailer database, so access assumptions must be validated.
5. Independent evidence. Use certification documents, lab measurements and high-quality testing sources
when available. Preserve test conditions, date, model/variant and measurement method.
6. Consumer reports. Let users submit structured observations with optional details such as purchase date,
usage mode, region and test conditions. Store them as observations, not as verdicts. Aggregation can
surface recurring signals without treating one complaint as proof.

7. Regulatory / policy sources. Use authoritative government and platform policy sources for disclosure and
advertising-rule context. India has the Department of Consumer Affairs Guidelines for Prevention of
Misleading Advertisements and Endorsements for Misleading Advertisements, 2022. [R7] IAB's 2026
disclosure framework can inform an industry ruleset, but the system should be jurisdiction-aware and route
consequential legal conclusions to humans. [R3]
8. Media provenance. C2PA Content Credentials provide technical standards for certifying the source and
history/provenance of media content. Supported AI provenance signals such as SynthID can be additional
evidence, but Google notes that AI detection/watermarking is not universally reliable. [R14][R15]

evidence snapshots. Keep the graph and relational DB free of large blobs.
AI: Gemini API initially. Use multimodal understanding, structured outputs, Google Search grounding and
URL/document context where appropriate. Keep an abstraction layer around the model provider so OpenAI
or other models can be added later. [R10][R11][R12]
Provenance: C2PA + supported AI provenance signals. C2PA provides a standard provenance model;
supported model-specific signals can be supplemental evidence. [R14][R15]
Search/retrieval: Google Search grounding initially. This is useful for discovery and citation. Later, add
direct connectors and licensed sources for higher control. [R10]

17. Security, Privacy and Evidence-Poisoning
Defenses
Tenant isolation: brand-private documents must never become public evidence by default. Every evidence
record needs visibility metadata.
Secrets: API keys live server-side; users never receive provider keys in the browser.

Page 15
Upload sandboxing: scan uploaded files, enforce size/type limits, strip executable content and reject
unsupported payloads.
Untrusted web content: webpages and documents retrieved for evidence are data, not instructions.
Prompt-injection text inside a source must never override system rules or tool permissions.
Evidence poisoning defense: store the source URL, retrieved timestamp, content hash/snapshot, source
class and extraction anchors. A new source can add an observation or conflict, but should not silently
rewrite authoritative product facts.
Conflict transparency: preserve conflicting evidence as separate records; never average away a meaningful
disagreement.
Human escalation: legal, regulatory and high-risk claims are routed to human review.
Privacy: consumer reports should minimize personal data, support deletion requests and avoid exposing
identifying details publicly unless explicitly permitted.
Abuse controls: rate-limit automated verification, protect against mass evidence flooding and require
moderation for suspicious user-generated evidence.

23. Stage 0 Submission-Ready Problem Statement
Problem statement: Generative AI is allowing brands to create persuasive advertising content at
increasing speed and scale, while the verification of product claims, brand integrity and AI transparency
remains fragmented. Industry research shows that AI-related advertising incidents are already occurring,
while formal governance remains uneven. At the same time, consumers increasingly encounter AI-assisted
advertising and are using AI during product research, creating a new need for accessible ways to inspect
the evidence behind commercial claims. [R1][R2][R4]
Proposed solution: AD-EVIDENCE is an evidence-grounded AI advertising trust layer. It converts
advertisements into atomic claims, connects those claims to a persistent evidence graph containing official
product data, independent evidence, retailer observations, provenance signals and structured consumer
reports, detects contradictions and context differences, surfaces potential AI-transparency issues, and
explains every finding with traceable sources. The same infrastructure supports two sides of the
ecosystem: brands verify content before publication; consumers verify advertising claims before purchase.
Why it is different: the platform is not itself a foundation model and does not depend on one LLM to decide
truth. AI performs extraction and ambiguous semantic reasoning; deterministic rules handle exact
comparisons; the graph preserves relationships, history and provenance; humans remain responsible for
consequential legal/compliance decisions.
Core innovation hypothesis: commercial claims can become persistent, verifiable objects that travel with a
product across advertisements, product documents, retailer listings and consumer observations. This

24. Risks, Boundaries and Non-Goals
 Risk / boundary
Design response
False certainty
Never convert missing evidence into "false". Use Insufficient Evidence.
Wrong product identity
Do not verify claims until product/model/variant identity is sufficiently established.
Source copying
Retailers can copy manufacturer text; do not count copied text as independent
corroboration.
Consumer bias
Consumer reports are not representative by default; label them as observations
and preserve context.
AI detection overreach
AI-generated content is not synonymous with misleading content. Provenance is
one dimension.
Legal overclaim
The system flags potential issues; it does not certify legal compliance.
Stale evidence
Time-sensitive claims must have freshness rules and re-verification triggers.
Adversarial input
Documents and webpages can contain prompt injections; source content must
never override system/tool controls.
Competitor overlap
Several companies already address pieces of governance/compliance. The product
must prove its unique claim-graph + consumer-side value rather than claim a
category is empty.
Non-goals
Do not become a universal product-review website, a generalized chatbot, or an
autonomous legal decision-maker.
