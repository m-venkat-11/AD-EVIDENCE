 SAMARTHA - STAGE 0 PROJECT CONCEPT
 AD-EVIDENCE
 The AI Advertising Trust Layer
Core idea
A two-sided verification layer for the AI advertising era: marketing teams can validate
claims before publishing, and consumers can verify claims when they encounter an
advertisement before purchasing.
Central problem
Generative AI can scale persuasive advertising far faster than traditional human review
can scale. This creates a risk that unsupported, exaggerated, contradictory, off-brand or
insufficiently disclosed claims reach consumers.
Key difference
AD-EVIDENCE is not another general AI chatbot. Its value comes from a structured
evidence layer, claim-level provenance, brand/product knowledge, rules, uncertainty
handling and a shared workflow for both brands and consumers.
Working project title: AD-EVIDENCE - The AI Advertising Trust Layer
This document is intended for team discussion and Stage 0 submission development. The research statistics are the latest published sources located
for this document as of September 2026; they are not live real-time telemetry.

1. Problem Statement - In Simple Terms
Advertising is becoming easier to create with generative AI. A marketing team can generate many versions of an
advertisement, including text, images, video, product descriptions and social posts. The difficult part is no longer just
producing the content; it is proving that the content is accurate, consistent with the brand, properly disclosed and
supported by evidence.
For consumers, the same issue appears from the opposite direction. A shopper can see a polished advertisement
containing a numerical claim, comparison, guarantee, testimonial or product image, but may have no convenient way to
check what the advertisement is actually supported by. This becomes more important as consumers also use generative
AI to research and compare products.
Core problem: There is a growing gap between the speed and volume of AI-assisted marketing and the
ability to verify the claims and representations reaching consumers.

What the Statistics Mean
70% incidents: IAB surveyed 125 U.S. advertising executives in July 2024. More than 70% reported at least one
AI-related advertising incident, including hallucinations, bias, inappropriate/off-brand outputs or compliance issues. This
does not mean 70% of all ads are misleading. [1]
40% paused/pulled ads: In the same study, 40% said AI-related incidents led them to pause or pull advertisements. This
shows operational consequences for the surveyed organizations. [1]
83% creative-process adoption: IAB's 2026 study used 104 U.S. ad-industry executives and found 83% said their
company had deployed AI in the creative process, up from 60% in the 2024 comparison study. [2]
71% believed they had seen an AI ad: This came from the 505 Gen Z/Millennial U.S. consumers in the same IAB study.
It is a perception/exposure measure, not proof that 71% of all consumers encounter AI ads. [2]
73% disclosure effect: In the same sample, 73% said knowing an ad was created with AI would either increase or not
change purchase likelihood. This supports exploring transparent verification rather than assuming that AI disclosure
always reduces trust. [2]
67% in India: Ipsos surveyed 23,000 people across 30 countries, including India, and reported that 67% of Indians
expected products/services using AI to disclose that use. [6]
Implications for AD-EVIDENCE
The data points to two connected needs. On the brand side, teams need governance that can scale with AI-assisted
content production. On the consumer side, shoppers need transparent ways to inspect product claims as AI-generated
advertising becomes more common and AI itself becomes part of the shopping journey.
The opportunity is therefore not to build another AI writer. It is to create a trust layer between advertising
claims and the evidence supporting them.
This framing also prevents a common mistake: treating "AI-generated" as synonymous with "false." A human-created ad
can contain a misleading claim, and an AI-created ad can contain an accurate claim. AD-EVIDENCE should therefore treat
AI provenance as one signal within a broader evidence-based verification process.

3. Proposed Solution
AD-EVIDENCE is a specialized AI advertising trust layer that connects marketing claims to evidence. It is designed to
answer two related questions:
For brands: "Can we publish this marketing content confidently, or are there claims/representations that need
review?"
For consumers: "What exactly is this advertisement claiming, what evidence supports those claims, and what
remains uncertain?"
The product should not return a single unexplained "truth score." Instead, it should produce an explainable,
claim-by-claim verification report. The system should clearly distinguish between supported claims, contradictions and
cases where there is not enough evidence.

8. Why Not Just Upload the Product Document to GPT or Gemini?
That is a valid challenge, and it exposes the main weakness of a generic version of this idea. If AD-EVIDENCE only sends

10. Existing Market Solutions - and the Gap We Need to Own
The idea is not an empty category. Adobe GenStudio already provides brand governance, brand rules, compliance
validation and approval workflows. Persado provides AI-assisted marketing compliance and audit trails. Therefore we
should not claim that nobody is solving advertising governance.
Our proposed gap is narrower: a shared claim/evidence verification layer that deliberately serves both sides of the
market and explains the evidence behind every finding. For Stage 0, this should be presented as a focused product
hypothesis that we want to validate, not as a claim that all competitors lack these capabilities.

11. Why the Consumer Side Is Important
The consumer side makes the concept more than an internal compliance dashboard. BCG's January 2026 research
reported that shopping-related GenAI use grew 35% between February and November 2025 and that more than 60% of
surveyed consumers expressed high trust in GenAI results. BCG also reports that GenAI can be highly influential in
purchase journeys, which makes trustworthy product information increasingly important at the moment of decision. [4]
The consumer experience should therefore focus on verification, not persuasion. It should not tell a shopper which
product to buy. It should show what each advertisement claims, what evidence supports those claims, what conflicts exist
and what remains uncertain, so the consumer can make the decision themselves.
12. Regulatory and Industry Context
India has an established consumer-protection framework that includes the 2022 Guidelines for Prevention of Misleading
Advertisements and Endorsements. [7] This does not mean our system can automatically determine legal compliance; it
does mean there is a real policy context around misleading advertising.
IAB published Version 2 of its AI Transparency & Disclosure Framework on August 18, 2026. The framework uses a
risk-based, materiality-driven approach and addresses AI-generated and AI-assisted text, imagery, video, audio, synthetic
voices, digital twins and AI-powered consumer interactions. [3]
Google's July 2026 policy update says certain AI-generated or AI-edited advertising assets may need disclosures/labels
under emerging rules affecting India and explicitly warns that its labeling feature does not guarantee legal compliance. [5]
This supports an important product principle: AD-EVIDENCE should flag potential issues for human review, not promise
universal legal certification.

13. Recommended Stage 0 Problem Statement
Problem: Generative AI is enabling brands to create marketing content at unprecedented speed and scale, but the ability
to verify product claims, preserve brand integrity and handle AI transparency has not automatically scaled with content
production. Industry research shows that AI-related advertising incidents are already occurring, while governance and
disclosure practices are still evolving. At the consumer end, shoppers are increasingly encountering AI-assisted marketing
and using AI during product research, yet they lack a simple, evidence-based way to inspect the claims made by
advertisements. [1][2][3][4][6]
Proposed solution: AD-EVIDENCE - The AI Advertising Trust Layer - is an evidence-grounded verification system that
extracts material claims from advertising, connects those claims to authoritative or brand-approved evidence, detects
contradictions and rule conflicts, identifies potential transparency/disclosure issues, and produces an explainable report.
The same trust layer can support marketing teams before publication and consumers after encountering an advertisement.
14. Product Vision

15. Boundaries - What We Should Not Claim
 Do not claim that AI-generated advertising is inherently misleading.
 Do not claim that a model can guarantee legal compliance across jurisdictions.
 Do not present a generated "trust score" as objective truth without evidence.
 Do not treat social/media signals as representative of all consumers.
 Do not claim that AD-EVIDENCE is the first or only product in advertising governance; existing enterprise tools already
address parts of this space.
